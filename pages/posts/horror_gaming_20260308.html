Horror Gaming Hiatus

I recently got a taste for spooky games and got a little addicted to the thrill of being scared :3

Why

Recently, I had an (admittedly small) incursion into horror games. I’ve never been particularly good with horror in any media. As a result, I’ve never really played much specifically horror games. I think the only exceptions to this would be things like Resident Evil 4 and Left 4 Dead 2, both of which I suppose are more action oriented.

What spurred this on was playing the Call of Cthulu: Dark Corners of the Earth game. Someone wanted to watch me play it after reminiscing having played some horror game as a kid and quitting a couple minutes in because they were too scared lol. Although this game was kinda cheesy, there were still plenty sections that were spooky and I kinda liked the vibe so I wanted more :3. I specifically asked a friend for recommendations and they suggested Penumbra; a game/series I had literally never heard of or seen prior. This is what led me to playing mostly their games during this. Frictional Games are best known for the Amnesia series, which I had heard of but never played.

Despite still really struggling (I’m a little baby), I was hooked to the feeling I got while playing these :D I guess it was just new and the stories and atmosphere of these games was enough to keep me trying even when I was cowering in a corner hiding from monsters ><

It would appear this small horror hiatus was short lived, however as I haven’t really played any for a bit over a month now. I fear the high quality of these initial entries to my repertoire have me spoiled and simply uninterested in many of the other options out there. Nonetheless, I had fun playing these and streaming them to friends ><

What

As I said, for the most part I just played Frictional Games’ works. What started it off though was Call of Cthulu: Dark Corners of the Earth.

Call of Cthulu: Dark Corners of the Earth

Call of Cthulu had a lot of interesting things going for it but was overall quite cheesy haha. The game opens with you investigating a cult who have requested a meeting with you— Jack Walters— by name. Long story short, you find some crazy shit in the basement and lose consciousness. Fast forward, Jack has no recollection of the past six years since the night at the cult hideout, the entirety of which he has spent completely insane in an asylum. The rest of the game follows you being tasked with investigating a missing person in the small fishing town of Innsmouth.

The voice acting is consistently cheesy throughout the game, the worst offender being the lead, Jack, funnily enough. The townsfolk of Innsmouth all look and sound sufficiently creepy however. The gun play has no business being as satisfying as it is in this game. It’s really quite enjoyable once you get your hands on a few guns :3. This game also has a very interesting take on a health system; each of Jack’s body parts can be injured individually and in various ways, the healing of which requires a variety of different items depending on the type and severity of said injuries.

I don’t think it’s ever explained to you (or if it was I missed it) but there’s a hidden sanity mechanic, by which if Jack witnesses too many negative or horrific things (hell, even just looking down from too high up because apparently Jack is afraid of heights lmao (..same)), he takes a hit to his sanity. Now, again, I feel like this was never actually explained so the entire game I was confused why Jack kept panting like a rabid dog and whispering to himself lmao but ye turns out that was his sanity getting dangerously low.. and I only realised this about three-quarters through the game when— and I shit you not— Jack stopped dead in his tracks to pull out his gun and shoot himself!?!?

Penumbra

Overture

In hindsight, this game was kinda basic. The premise was alluring enough though with a good opening to hook you. The game also had a very funny character named Red that keeps you intrigued and wanting to progress. A lot of the game’s lore and story is actually not directly fed to the player but rather found through reading of letters, papers etc. throughout the environment. You’ll find this in all the Frictional Games it seems; they don’t force much down your neck and you’re expected to figure things out for yourself otherwise you may just get a very basic interpretation of the story. Between me screaming at spiders (yes there’s spiders) and reading every article I found, my throat was actually sore after playing this one lmao.

Gameplay wise, these are heavily centred around solving puzzles, with some basic inventory management, crafting and the Newton Game Dynamics’ physics coming into play from time to time. Often the solutions or hints to said puzzles being found in the aforementioned notes and environmental hints. The areas in the game are little more than a series of corridor grid layouts, one after another where you have to find specific items to progress (hey I play doom, I can’t complain. Just thought it’s worth mentioning). It rarely deviates from this.

The combat in Overture is nothing special either and frankly sometimes a detriment to the experience. I suppose the intention was you aren’t really meant to engage the enemies and as a first entry they just felt they had to have combat in here as the later games for the most part don’t have combat at all. I often got rather frustrated trying to stun lock the wolves only to have it fail for no reason and I’d die because of it. It’s primarily a stealth game and on that note the game is meant to be dark. There are a lot of lighting type items such as flares and batteries for your flash light to collect but for my play through I rarely needed to use these because the gamma setting they insist you set appropriately just doesn’t succeed in making the game dark.

I would recommend this one only because it sets up the second episode and the story of Red and general intrigue the game creates is worth it. Overall it hasn’t aged well though.

Black plague

Continuing directly after the first episode, Black Plague was considerably scarier I found.

This game had the same brightness problem the first one had. This time around though I managed to fix it with a gamma shader and it made the game far more enjoyable and scary ><

There’s not much more to say other than it’s like the first one but way better. The environments, although still kind of linear corridor grids I found felt a lot more interesting and thought out than the first episode. The main enemies in Black Plague were really creepy and there’s a couple scripted encounters early on that horrified me lmao.

There were, however two major gripes I had with the game. They tried to have another omnipresent character again, akin to Red radioing you constantly in the first game. The Black Plague equivalent— Clarence— just pissed me off haha. I definitely think that was the point; they’re meant to be messing with you. That’s their shtick.

There is another character whom you make contact with via computers found throughout the game— Dr. Amabel Swanson— whom I was really looking forward to finally meeting as you spend a good portion of the game trying to get to her. Right as you finally get to her, Clarence tricks you into killing her… To say I was genuinely pissed off would be an understatement.

The ending was pretty interesting but I may have been slightly annoyed at that last thing still. Along with the final area being just weird, the ending kind of went over my head. The lore intrigued me enough though that I watched some videos after, hoping to find out more and realised it was pretty good actually.

soma

Soma’s premise and story were fantastic. The setup for the game has a cool, multi faceted approach to story telling that I haven’t experienced much before. Without spoiling too much (told you I’d try ><), the events of Soma are brought about by an apocalyptic event and you’re only experiencing them due to something’s reaction to them. Similar to Frictional Game’s other games, not much of anything is forced on you so there’s a lot of moments in the game where things about the story and background will occur to you in a satisfying way based on what you find and how much you’re paying attention. Soma has quite a bit of body horror as well as repeatedly touching on themes of existentialism.[^1]

[1]: I'm not sure if existentialism is the right word here haha but the game constantly poses the question of what it means to be human and, given the events of the game, does anything you're doing throughout the game even mean anything.

One of the first things I noticed early on was just how phenomenal the sound design is in Soma. Even in the opening apartment and office scenes— where there’s not much environmental sounds— it’s immediately noticeable how immersive all the sounds are. This only gets better once you’re put into the main part of the game. The constant creaking and groaning of the underwater structures under the crushing weight of the ocean, your footsteps as well as enemies’ reverberating through the halls and station machinery and equipment in disrepair near and far. Noteworthy even down to how great it sounds crawling through one of the many vents and crawl spaces. All making for a very eerie atmosphere.

Really the only negative thing I can say about Soma is; there isn’t any combat to speak of (yeah, one of those horror games). It’s really just about the story and experience.

Amnesia: The Bunker

And in contrast to that is Amnesia: The Bunker. This game, to many, was considered a return to form for Frictional Games gameplay wise. Set during World War I, inside a..— you guessed it— bunker. Amnesia The Bunker has you trying to escape an underground bunker on the war front that’s been decimated by some monstrosity. All the while, said monster is hunting you! And if that wasn’t bad enough, the greatest horror of all.. you’re French lmao (I joke >< coucou x).

This game was more mechanically rich than the others. It had such a variety of choices in how to approach things and interactions with certain objects, it almost feels like an immersive-sim at times. There’s some basic crafting and besides just looking for items required to progress, you have to scavenge for items necessary for survival as well as deterring the monster.

I played on the hardest difficulty available at the start and resources were fairly scarce but I never felt like I couldn’t manage. Once you complete the game you unlock shell shock difficulty and the ability to customise the difficulty to your liking. My favourite settings are the option to make the safe room accessible to the monster and saves requiring fuel. With the diversity of gameplay along with customisable difficulty settings, this is by far the most re-playable of the games I played here. One thing to note about the difficulty etc. is that the monster feels somewhat omnipresent throughout the game and will just come out seemingly at will sometimes despite your best efforts to stay quiet. How egregious this is is definitely tied to the difficulty level but it just felt a bit meta-gamey at times.

This one had less of an over-arching story however and although there were some connections to the other Amnesia games this really does only feel like an Amnesia game in spirit.

There’s also one minor grievance I had with the game and that’s that there’s these weird, little loading sections between the areas of the bunker. They’re not actual loading screens but just a little stutter and visual artifact as you cross over into a different area.. it’s the very fact they’re so brief that begs the question why they’re there at all. Like I thought this was a solved issue in games haha. Especially such linear ones like this.

Attempts

There was one game I tried and didn’t finish and that was Signalis. This one just didn’t enthral me like the others and I think it was simply down to the perspective. I’ve heard the story and premise are very good however so I may return to this one in the future.

Future

In the future, I definitely want to play more horror games however for now I feel I’ve had my fill. I suppose the obvious next choice would be the rest of the Amnesia series. There was also Total Chaos; a mod for Doom2 that looked really promising and there’s even a full release of it not made in GZDoom. Another recommendation from a friend was Visage; another game I’ve never heard of but looks pretty good but not much else I can say at this point.
